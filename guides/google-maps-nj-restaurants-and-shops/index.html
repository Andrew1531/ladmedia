How New Jersey restaurants and shops show up in Google Maps

Restaurants & retail · November 25, 2026 · 5 min read · By Andrew, LAD Media, Newark, NJ

When someone nearby searches for "pizza near me" or "gift shop Montclair", Google Maps decides which places they see first. Three things drive it: how relevant your profile is to the search, how close you are, and how well known your business is. You cannot move your location, but you control the rest. This guide covers what a walk-in business needs before anything else.

Get the basics right first

These fields decide which searches you can appear in. Check them in your Google Business Profile.

  1. Primary category

    Be specific: "Italian restaurant" or "Coffee shop" beats "Restaurant", and "Bicycle store" beats "Store". Add secondary categories only for what you really offer.

  2. Hours, including holidays

    Set regular hours and add special hours before every holiday, snow day and summer change. Wrong hours lead to bad reviews faster than anything else.

  3. Address and pin

    Make sure the map pin sits on your actual entrance, especially in a strip mall or a building with several storefronts.

  4. Phone and website

    Use a number that is answered during opening hours, and link to your own website rather than a third-party page.

What walk-in customers look for

People choosing where to go right now decide on what they can see in a few seconds:

  1. Your menu or products

    Restaurants: add the menu in your profile or link to a menu page on your site, not a PDF. Shops: add your main products with photos and price ranges.

  2. Real photos

    The storefront from the street, the inside, your best dishes or products, and your team. Add a few every month so the profile looks current.

  3. Attributes

    Mark what applies, such as outdoor seating, takeout, delivery, wheelchair-accessible entrance or Wi-Fi. These answer questions before people call.

  4. Ordering and booking links

    If you take online orders or reservations, add the links so customers can act straight from Maps.

Reviews and replies

Reviews affect both where you appear and whether people choose you. Ask every customer, by a QR code at the register or on the receipt, and never offer anything in return.

Reply to every review within a few days. Short, specific thanks for good reviews, and a calm reply with a way to follow up offline for bad ones.

Where to start this week

Check your primary category, hours and map pin today. Then add ten real photos and your menu or top products.

After that, a routine of a few photos and one update a week keeps the profile active. Measure calls, direction requests and website clicks in your profile over a few months.
